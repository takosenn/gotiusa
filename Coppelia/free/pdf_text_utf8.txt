Abstract—In this study, distributed circle surrounding
control problems are investigated for double-integrator
anonymous multiagent systems, where all available infor-
mation for each agent can be obtained based on its moving
local coordinate frame. First, the dynamics of each agent
described in the local frames are developed in light of
the transformation relation between the global and local
frames. Then, distributed formation control protocols are
designed by local measurements to form and maintain the
expected arbitrary circle formations in a plane. The pro-
posed protocols efﬁciently reduce the requirement of the
equipped devices and data processing capabilities, since
the agents do not need to be equipped with expensive and
high-precision sensors to acquire the real-time acceleration
of the target. Finally, the effectiveness of the theoretical
result is demonstrated by simulations.

B. Related Papers
Formation control problems have been studied from three
perspectives. Following the development of consensus theory,
consensus-based formation control problems have been pro-
moted greatly in the past ten years [16], [17], [18], [19], [20],
[21]. It should be noted that the acquisition of state information
in this context depends on the axis alignment of the coordinate
frames of all agents. To overcome the above problem, graph
rigidity theory is introduced to accomplish the desired formation
by relative distance information without relying on a global or
common coordinate system [22], [23], [24], [25]. Different from
the above two cases, the general task of the circle formation
control problems is usually divided into two subtasks. The ﬁrst
is target circling. In other words, each agent keeps the same
desired angular velocity around the moving/stationary target
and maintains the desired distance from the target. The next is
distribution adjustment, which means that each agent maintains
desired relative angular distances from its two neighbors, respec-
tively. This study focuses on the case of circle formation control.
Hence, some existing results on this problem are reviewed in
detail in the following context.
In general, circle formation control problems are classiﬁed
into two cases according to the state variables. In the case of
state variables with respect to angles in a circle, most existing
results mainly studied the second subobjective. In [26] and [27],
sampled-data-based control laws to achieve circle formations
were proposed for single- and double-integrator MASs. The
follow-up investigations were carried out from different con-
strained conditions including unidirectional locomotion [28],
measurement errors [29], discontinuous updates [30], and lim-
ited interaction range [31]. By contrast, both subobjectives
can be achieved in the case with taking positions as the state
variables in a plane. For single-integrator MASs, Zhao and

difﬁcult or impossible to accurately acquire the target’s veloc-

formation control protocols are difﬁcult to implement in real
applications.
C. Contributions
With the above discussions, we mainly focus on the circle
surrounding formation problems of double-integrator MASs
in this study. Inspired by [35], a novel control scheme is
designed to drive each agent to form and maintain desired
formations. The contributions of this study are concluded as
follows.
1) New dynamical equations that describe the agents’ dy-
namics in its local coordinate frame are constructed

Speciﬁcally, all state variables can be acquired by direct
measurements or indirect calculations.
2) The proposed controllers are totally distributed, which
means that only local measurements from each agent’s
neighbors are required through their local coordinate
frames.
3) The designed control protocols do not depend on accurate
and real-time acceleration measurements of the target.
In particular, adaptive laws are constructed such that the
parameters of controller design are independent of any
global information, such as the upper bound of the target’s
acceleration.

V. CONCLUSION
This article studied the distributed circle surrounding control
problems for double-integrator MASs. Distributed formation
control protocols have been proposed to form and maintain the
expected arbitrary circle formations in a plane. The proposed
protocols do not rely on the target’s acceleration information in
real time, and thus, can efﬁciently reduce the requirement of the

equipped devices and data processing capabilities. In addition,
the dynamics of each agent described in the local frames were
developed by using the transformation relation between the
global and local frames. As a result, all the information used in
the controller design of each agent can be obtained by measuring
or calculating from its local coordinate frame. In conclusion, the
proposed design framework can be ﬂexibly employed according
to speciﬁc requirements. In future, we will focus on extending
the results to the case that each agent only can obtain relative
position information with the target and its neighbors.
